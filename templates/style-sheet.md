## Reader and voice
Reader, reading level, person (I / we / impersonal), tone, sentence habits.

House defaults:
- **Voicing views.** State ruled readings, and points the sources agree on,
  in the book's own voice, with the ledger or card link. Name a source only
  where the sources differ, or when quoting one. At most one sentence per
  paragraph built around a source ("X says…"). Explain the point; don't pass
  it from source to source. (house default)
- **The answer is findable.** Each unit states its answer to its question (or
  the headword's core sense, or the lesson's main point) where a reader can
  find it and say it back in one sentence. Name the section where it goes.
  (house default)

## Terminology
Terms the tradition uses, and generic terms to avoid in their place. Names of
God, people, places and books as this book spells them. Wording the ledger
fixes (for example a ruling on how to name a group of people).

House default:
- **Source names.** A table of every registered source: full name at first
  mention in each unit, then one fixed short name. Never use one short name

## Scripture
Base translation, how quotations appear, reference format, what to do where
the base translation and the original differ.

House default:
- Where the commentary's reading departs from the base translation's
  wording, say so and credit that wording to the translation. (house default)

## Greek and Hebrew
Where they appear (body, notes, not at all), script only and never
transliterated, gloss format, how much parsing a reader sees.

House defaults:
- Gloss every time the word appears, including in headings. (house default)
- Forms, lemmas and glosses only from dataset output on a source card. Where
  the data gives no parsing, say nothing about it or write "not in source
  data". (house default)
- Show word order only when an argument depends on it. (house default)

## Other views
How views outside the tradition are named and stated, per the charter. How
unruled questions are laid out (no lean in framing, word choice or which
sources are cited), and what weight alternatives get where Ben has ruled.

## Application
Whether units end with application (for example a closing paragraph for the
preacher), how long, and what it may draw on. House default where the charter
asks for application: draw only on ruled or agreed material, never on a
question left open. (house default)

## Citations and abbreviations
The authorities the charter names (for example SBL Handbook, Chicago) and any
house exceptions. Claim links stay in drafts until production.

House default: claim links are a card id, a ledger id, [BT] for the base
translation, or [Charter: <clause>] for a sentence that states one of the
charter's own positions. (house default)
