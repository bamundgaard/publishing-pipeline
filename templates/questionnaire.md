# Intake questionnaire

## Product
1. Resource type and genre template (commentary, Bible dictionary, theological or preaching dictionary, study guide, background resource)
2. Scope: books, passages, topics or headwords; target length and length tiers
3. Primary reader (pastor, lay leader, student, scholar) and reading level
4. Output formats and any downstream platform or data it must feed

## Theological frame
5. Tradition, and the confessional document that sets its boundaries
6. Mode: argue a position within the tradition, or survey views neutrally
7. Treatment of other traditions: named and steelmanned, mentioned briefly, or omitted
8. Positions on critical questions (authorship, date, historicity, canon), or "survey these"
9. Sensitive topics and how each must be handled; any required ecclesial approval

## Texts and languages
10. Base translation, and whether its license allows quotation at the planned volume
11. Original-language texts (e.g. NA28 or SBLGNT, BHS or BHQ, a Septuagint edition)
12. Language depth: Greek and Hebrew in the body, in notes, or not at all; script and gloss policy

## Sources
13. Allowed corpus: licensed titles, public domain works, Aquifer, other open resources; explicit exclusions
14. Works that must be engaged for this book or topic
15. Recency requirements and citation style

## Authorship and voice
16. Named human author or editor, and which section types the human writes, dictates or only revises
17. Voice samples, person and tone
18. Disclosure statement for AI involvement

## Review and time
19. Human reviewers beyond the author; who holds final theological sign-off
20. Human time budget per gate (sets how much is sampled versus read in full)

## Rights and release
21. License of the output, permissions budget, release and errata plan
